SS430 sensitisation via DL-EPR

Double Loop Electrochemical Potentiokinetic Reactivation (DL-EPR) is one of the cleaner ways to quantify sensitisation in stainless steels without destroying the sample. SS430 — a ferritic grade — has thinner literature than 304/316, which makes it slightly more interesting to work with.

Context

Sensitisation in ferritic grades occurs at different temperature ranges compared to austenitic steels. Chromium carbide and nitride precipitation at grain boundaries depletes the adjacent matrix — but the kinetics are faster and the recovery behaviour is different. DL-EPR catches this by measuring the ratio of the reactivation current to the activation current (Ir/Ia). Higher ratio = more sensitised.

Setup

Standard electrolyte: 0.5 M H₂SO₄ + 0.01 M KSCN, room temperature. Scan rate: 1.667 mV/s. Potential sweep from −500 mV vs SCE to +200 mV, then reversed back to start.

Specimens were heat-treated in two conditions before testing:

· ~600 °C / 2 h — borderline sensitisation range
· 750 °C / 2 h — expected higher sensitisation

Surface preparation: ground to 1200 grit, finished with 1 µm diamond suspension. Rinsed with ethanol, dried in air. Mounted face-down in the cell immediately.

Observed

Reactivation peak in SS430 is sharper and narrower than in austenitic grades — the loop closes faster. This seems consistent with the faster diffusion kinetics in the ferritic matrix.

At ~600 °C / 2 h: Ir/Ia below 0.05 — borderline. Hard to call definitively sensitised at this condition.

At 750 °C / 2 h: jumped to ~0.18. Much more convincing. Grain boundary attack visible in post-test optical inspection.

Notes

Residual grinding marks from coarser grit gave irreproducible loops on early runs — probably preferential attack at surface defects inflating the reactivation peak. Always finish with 1 µm diamond. This is not optional.

KSCN concentration is sensitive. Weigh it properly — a small error shifts the reactivation peak shape noticeably. Mixed 1 L batches, used within 48 h.

The 600 °C condition needs more repeat runs. Three specimens, two outliers. Suspect surface prep variability. To be continued.
