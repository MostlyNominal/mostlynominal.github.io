SS430 Sensitisation via DL-EPR

Double-loop electrochemical potentiokinetic reactivation — DL-EPR — is one of the cleaner ways to quantify sensitisation in stainless steels without sectioning. You scan anodic, hold, then scan back cathodic. The ratio of reactivation peak current (Ir) to activation peak current (Ia) gives you a sensitisation degree. Simple in principle. Less simple in practice.

Material and condition

Grade SS430. Ferritic. 17 wt% Cr, no intentional stabilisation. Supplied as sheet, cold-rolled. Two conditions tested: as-received, and sensitised by isothermal anneal at 700 °C / 1 h followed by air cool. The anneal puts you squarely in the sensitisation window for ferritic grades — carbide and nitride precipitation at grain boundaries, chromium depletion around them.

Test setup

Electrolyte: 0.5 M H₂SO₄ + 0.01 M KSCN at room temperature. KSCN suppresses repassivation on the reverse scan, which sharpens the reactivation peak and improves sensitivity. Three-electrode cell: working electrode (sample), Ag/AgCl reference, Pt counter. Scan rate: 1.67 mV/s. Activation scan from −0.6 V to +0.3 V vs. OCP; reactivation scan back to −0.6 V.

Surface prep: SiC grind to 1200 grit, finish with 1 µm diamond suspension, degrease with acetone and IPA, dry under N₂. Masked with lacquer to expose a defined area — nominally 1 cm².

What happened

The sensitised sample gave a clear reactivation peak as expected. The as-received sample — theoretically not sensitised — also showed a small but repeatable reactivation peak. Ir/Ia around 0.04. Low, but non-zero. Three repeats, same result. Not noise.

Two possibilities. First: the as-received material already has some degree of sensitisation from the rolling and any thermal history in processing. Ferritic grades sensitise faster than austenitics and at lower temperatures — you don't need much. Second: surface prep artefact. If the diamond polishing step isn't fully removing the deformed layer from grinding, you can get anomalous current contributions on the reverse scan.

Ran a quick check — electropolished one sample instead of mechanical polish. Reactivation peak dropped. Not eliminated, but dropped. So probably both factors contributing.

Where this is going

Need to rerun with electropolished surfaces as standard. Also want to try a shorter anneal — 30 min at 700 °C — to see if I can capture an intermediate sensitisation degree and get a better calibration of the Ir/Ia scale for this specific material lot. The KSCN concentration might also be worth varying; some literature suggests 0.005 M gives better peak resolution for ferritics.

Mostly nominal. Curve is behaving. Just need to understand what it's actually telling me before I trust it.
